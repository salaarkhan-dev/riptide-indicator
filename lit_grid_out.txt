================================================================================================
  READ
================================================================================================
  DECAY is the number to look at. A large positive in-sample delta
  that collapses out-of-sample is the signature of picking the best
  of many draws, and it is what a grid produces from pure noise.

  'tuning advantage OOS' is the whole claim in one number: how much
  better per-pair tuning did on data it was NOT picked on. If it is
  at or below zero, the settings that looked better were fitted to
  the window they were chosen from.

  Nothing here can change LIT_FORWARD_V1. It is frozen by
  PREREG_lit_forward_v1.md; a settings change would be V2 and would
  need its own pre-registration and its own out-of-sample test.

====================================================================================================
  INTERPRETATION  (written after the run)
====================================================================================================

THE GRID FOUND WINNERS. THEY DID NOT SURVIVE.

Per-pair tuning, which is the claim, decayed by ~0.95 R/bet on BOTH
timeframes - from +1.040 to +0.088 on Min15, and from +0.930 to -0.029 on
Min30. That is not a small shrinkage. It is nearly the whole effect.

A decay of that size is what a grid produces from noise. Each pair's champion
is the best of 162 draws; the in-sample number is the maximum of a
distribution, and the maximum of a distribution is mostly luck. The
out-of-sample number is what was actually there underneath.

THE TUNING ADVANTAGE, WHICH IS THE ONE NUMBER THE CLAIM REDUCES TO:

    Min15   +0.223   per-pair tuning beat the frozen default
    Min30   -0.635   per-pair tuning LOST to the frozen default

Opposite signs on two timeframes of the same experiment. If per-pair tuning
carried real information it would not flip sign between 15m and 30m on the
same symbols over the same bars. Two cells disagreeing this violently is what
"no signal, high variance" looks like.

THE SINGLE DEFAULT ALSO MOVED AROUND, AND THAT IS THE POINT.

The frozen default went -0.135 out-of-sample on Min15 and +0.606 on Min30.
Those swings are as large as anything the tuning produced. At n=146 and n=34
bets neither is a measurement of anything; the Min30 default's +0.606 on 34
bets is exactly as unreliable as the tuned numbers, and it would be dishonest
to quote it as evidence the default is good.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY. It does not say the settings are identical in effect,
or that structure settings never matter. It says that CHOOSING them per pair
by looking at past performance does not transfer, at this sample size, on this
data.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER. A per-pair rule chosen for a structural reason
rather than a performance reason - "this pair ticks coarsely, so Body
confirmation is the right filter" - decided BEFORE looking at returns, and
then tested out of sample. That is a different experiment, it would be
LIT_FORWARD_V2, and it needs its own pre-registration.

STATUS. LIT_FORWARD_V1 is unchanged and still frozen: entry, stop, Active
Price, exits and structure settings all exactly as pre-registered. This grid
changed nothing and was never able to.
